# HealthCheck Liver Health Risk — Methodology (v1)

**What this is:** a plain breakdown of how HealthCheck checks liver disease risk. One assessment, no lab needed, shown as a Low / Moderate / High band with the reasons behind it.

**In one line:** liver disease is common and mostly hidden in Nigeria. This check adds up the causes Nigerian liver doctors and hospital studies point to, and turns them into an early warning to get a simple liver and hepatitis test, while the damage can still be treated or reversed.

---

## 1. Why this matters, and why we built it this way

- Liver disease is a **silent killer**. It can move from inflammation to scarring (cirrhosis) to liver cancer over 20 to 30 years, during most of which a person feels well. By the time signs like yellow eyes appear, a lot of damage is already done.
- **Hepatitis B is the leading cause** and it is everywhere: about **1 in 10 Nigerians** carries it, most without knowing. It quietly drives cirrhosis and liver cancer, and Nigeria has one of the highest rates of hepatitis-B liver cancer in the world.
- The good news is that it is **treatable and preventable**: hepatitis B has a vaccine and medicine that stops the damage, hepatitis C can now be cured, and fatty liver reverses with weight and diet. The barrier is almost always **awareness and testing**.
- There is **no single no-lab liver score validated for Nigeria**. So we use the causes proven to matter here, show a band (not a made-up percentage), and always say a **liver function test plus hepatitis B and C screening** is what confirms things.

---

## 2. What the check looks at, and how much each counts

Each factor a person has adds points. Strongest first:

| Factor | Points | Why it is here |
|---|---|---|
| Hepatitis B or C (told positive) | 4 | The biggest cause of serious liver disease and cancer in Nigeria. |
| Alcohol (drinks regularly) | 3 | The top risk factor in Nigerian liver-disease admissions (about half of cases). |
| Herbal mixtures (agbo / paraga) | 2 | Found in nearly half of admissions; many are directly toxic to the liver. |
| Frequent painkillers (paracetamol) | 2 | Overusing or combining medicines is a leading cause of sudden liver damage. |
| Blood exposure | 2 | Transfusion, tribal marks, unsafe tattoos, shared blades, or unsafe injections spread hepatitis. |
| Family history | 2 | A close relative with liver disease or liver cancer. |
| Obesity (BMI 30+) | 2 | The main driver of fatty liver, which is rising fast in Nigeria. |
| Diabetes | 1 | Adds to fatty liver risk. |
| Sugary / processed diet | 1 | Soft drinks and processed food build fat in the liver. |
| Smoking | 1 | Found in about a third of Nigerian liver-disease admissions. |
| Never tested for hepatitis | 1 | Not a cause, but most carriers never know. A one-time test is the only way to be sure. |

**The band:**

- If the person **already knows they carry hepatitis B or C**, the band is **High**, because they need proper monitoring and treatment.
- Otherwise, **6 or more points** is **High**, **3 to 5** is **Moderate**, and **0 to 2** is **Low**.

---

## 3. Where each answer comes from

- **Already collected:** weight (BMI), alcohol, smoking, blood-sugar readings (for diabetes), and sugary-drink intake (from the diet questions).
- **New questions:** told you have hepatitis B or C, ever tested for hepatitis, family liver disease, and blood exposure (transfusion, tribal marks, tattoos, shared blades, unsafe injections).

If an answer is missing, it adds nothing, and the check still gives a usable band.

---

## 4. Worked examples

**A — 38-year-old man told he has hepatitis B.** Hepatitis alone → **High**, with a clear message to see a doctor, because medicine can stop it damaging his liver.

**B — 45-year-old who drinks regularly (3), takes agbo (2), and is obese (2).** Total 7 → **High**, even though he has no hepatitis and feels fine. Exactly the silent case doctors warn about.

**C — 29-year-old, no alcohol, no herbs, healthy weight, vaccinated, tested negative for hepatitis.** 0 points → **Low**.

---

## 5. The Nigerian evidence behind it

- A University of Nigeria Teaching Hospital (Enugu) study of liver-disease admissions (Nwokediuko et al.) found the main risk factors were **alcohol (52%), hepatitis B (49%), herbs and roots (45%), and smoking (30%)**, with liver cancer and cirrhosis making up about two thirds of cases. Our weights follow this order.
- Nigerian hepatologists (for example the SYNLAB Nigeria liver webinar) warn strongly about **self-medication**, especially painkiller and paracetamol misuse, combining medicines, and the false belief that herbal mixtures are safe. They stress early testing, hepatitis vaccination, and cutting alcohol.
- An estimated **20 million Nigerians** live with hepatitis B or C, most unaware.

## 7. Confidence and honest caveats (v1)

- **Grounded in Nigerian data:** the factors and their order follow a Nigerian hospital study and what liver doctors here report.
- **The band rule is a clear clinical judgement, not one proven equation:** how the factors add into Low / Moderate / High is a transparent rule we wrote and can show, so we show a band, never a false percentage.
- **Only a test confirms it, and even a normal test is not the full story:** liver doctors note that a normal liver function test does not completely rule out disease. A Low band is not a clean bill of health. Hepatitis B and C screening and, where needed, an ultrasound are what tell the real picture.
- **Watch the warning signs:** yellow eyes or skin, dark urine, a swollen belly, or long-lasting deep tiredness all need a doctor quickly. The app says this in the plan.
- **Always:** a HealthCheck result is a screening estimate, not a diagnosis, and the app says so.
